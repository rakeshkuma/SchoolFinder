There is no single "best school" in India; there is the best school **for your child and your family**. A school that is excellent for one child can be wrong for another because of distance, cost or the way it teaches.

This guide gives you a simple process, in five steps, and the questions that separate a good choice from a hopeful one.

## Step 1: Decide what matters to your family

Before you look at any school, write down your answers:

- **How far can your child travel each day?** Long journeys eat into rest, play and homework.
- **What can you afford every year, for every year?** Include tuition, transport, books, uniforms and activities, and expect increases.
- **Day school or boarding?** See our [day school vs boarding school guide](/blog/day-school-vs-boarding-school).
- **Which board?** See [CBSE vs ICSE](/blog/cbse-vs-icse). For most families, the school matters more than the board.
- **Anything your child specifically needs**, such as learning support, a sport or the arts.

## Step 2: Make a realistic shortlist

Search schools by **location, board and type**, then keep the ones within your distance and budget. On SchoolDhundo, you can [search the directory](/schools), look at [schools near you](/nearby), and filter by board and class.

Check that each school is **genuinely affiliated** to the board it names. For CBSE, the [SARAS directory](https://saras.cbse.gov.in/) lists affiliated schools with their affiliation numbers.

## Step 3: Compare what is published

For each school on your list, compare:

| What to compare | Where to find it |
| --- | --- |
| Classes offered, and up to which class | The school's website or board listing |
| Fees: the full annual cost | The school's published fee schedule |
| Admission dates and criteria | The school's admission notice |
| Facilities: library, labs, sports, transport | The school's website, and your visit |
| Teacher numbers and class sizes | Ask the school |

Our [compare tool](/compare) puts up to four schools side by side, and marks anything a school has not published as "Not available" rather than guessing.

## Step 4: Visit, and ask good questions

A visit tells you what no website can. Ask:

- How many children are in a class, and how many teachers per class?
- How does the school help a child who is struggling, or one who is ahead?
- How much homework is set, and how is it checked?
- How do parents and teachers stay in touch?
- What are the safety arrangements: gate, transport, CCTV where used, first aid, fire safety?
- Which fees are charged besides tuition, and how often do fees rise?

Notice how staff speak to children, whether classrooms are in use and cared for, and whether your questions get clear answers.

## Step 5: Read rankings and reviews carefully

- **Rankings** come from different publishers using different methods. A rank is one publisher's view for one year and category. On SchoolDhundo, every rank shows its publisher, year and category, and our [methodology page](/rankings/methodology) explains how we present them.
- **Reviews** are personal experiences. Look for patterns across many reviews, not single comments.
- No ranking or review can tell you whether a school suits **your** child. Use them to add schools to your list, not to decide.

## A note on admissions

For the entry classes of elementary education, the Right to Education Act does not allow schools to screen children or parents through tests or interviews; schools use published criteria such as distance, or a draw. Private unaided schools also reserve at least 25% of entry-level seats for children from economically weaker sections and disadvantaged groups, through a process run by the state. For higher classes, some schools hold a test.

## Your checklist

- Distance and daily travel time are manageable.
- The full annual cost fits your budget for the coming years.
- The school is affiliated to the board it names.
- The school goes up to the class (and stream) your child will need.
- You have visited, or spoken to current parents.
- You know the admission dates and documents. See our [documents checklist](/blog/documents-required-for-school-admission-in-india).
