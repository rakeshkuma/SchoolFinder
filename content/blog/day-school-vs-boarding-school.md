Sending a child to boarding school is a big family decision. Boarding can build independence and give access to schools far from home; day schooling keeps family life and support close. Neither is better for every child.

This article sets out the trade-offs honestly and gives you a checklist for any hostel you consider.

## The main differences

| | Day school | Boarding school |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily life | Home every evening, family routines | School routine around the clock, with house staff |
| Parent involvement | Daily, informal | Planned: visits, calls, holidays |
| Travel | Daily journey | Journeys at term start and end |
| Cost | Tuition and transport | Tuition plus hostel, mess and other charges |
| Independence | Grows gradually at home | Grows quickly through managing one's own day |
| Choice of schools | Limited to your area | Schools across the country |

## Signs a child may be ready for boarding

These are general signs, not rules:

- The child manages daily routines (waking, dressing, packing a bag) without much help.
- They are curious about the idea and, ideally, want to go.
- They can share space and settle disagreements with other children.
- They can tell an adult when something is wrong.

If your child is anxious, there is no harm in waiting a year, or choosing a **day-cum-boarding** school where the switch can come later.

## Government residential schools

Several government networks offer boarding, each with its own admission route:

- **Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas:** free education, boarding and lodging, with a small contribution from some students in Classes 9 to 12; admission to Class 6 through JNVST. Read our [JNV admission guide](/blog/jnv-admission-guide-jnvst-class-6).
- **Sainik Schools:** residential schools under the Ministry of Defence, through AISSEE; they charge fees, with scholarships for some students. See [how to prepare for AISSEE](/blog/sainik-school-entrance-exam-aissee-preparation).
- **Eklavya Model Residential Schools:** residential schools for Scheduled Tribe students, with admission rules set by each state.

**Kendriya Vidyalayas** are mostly **day schools**, not boarding schools.

> Fees, eligibility and admission dates for these schools change. Always check the current official notice before applying.

## What to check about any hostel

Ask the school, and check its prospectus, about:

- **Supervision:** who the wardens or house parents are, and how many children each looks after.
- **Health:** a nurse or doctor on campus, and the nearest hospital.
- **Safety:** how the campus is secured, fire safety, and separate arrangements for boys and girls.
- **Food:** menus, how often they change, and how dietary needs are handled.
- **Contact:** phone and visiting rules, and how quickly the school informs parents of problems.
- **Fees:** hostel, mess and any refundable deposit, which are often separate from tuition.
- **Study and rest:** supervised study hours, lights-out time, weekends and leave.

## Questions to ask yourself

1. Is boarding for my child's growth, or mainly for our family's convenience? Both are valid; be clear which.
2. Can we afford the full cost every year until the final class?
3. How will we stay close: calls, visits, holidays?
4. If boarding does not suit my child, what is our plan B?
