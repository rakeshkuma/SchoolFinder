Choosing a board is one of the first decisions parents face. The two national boards most families compare are the **Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE)** and the **Council for the Indian School Certificate Examinations (CISCE)**, which runs the **ICSE** (Class 10) and **ISC** (Class 12) examinations.

Both are recognised across India. This article explains how they differ, and then why the school often matters more than the board.

## Who runs each board

| | CBSE | CISCE (ICSE / ISC) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A national board under the Government of India's Ministry of Education | A national council established in 1958 that conducts the ICSE and ISC examinations |
| Class 10 examination | CBSE Class X board examination | ICSE (Indian Certificate of Secondary Education) |
| Class 12 examination | CBSE Class XII board examination | ISC (Indian School Certificate) |
| Textbooks | Most subjects follow NCERT textbooks | Schools choose books from publishers that follow the CISCE syllabus |
| Spread | Far more affiliated schools, in almost every district | Fewer schools, mostly in cities and towns |
| Official website | cbse.gov.in | cisce.org |

## How teaching differs

These are tendencies parents commonly report, not rules; individual schools differ widely.

- **CBSE** follows the NCERT curriculum closely in most subjects. Families who move often like that a child can change schools with less disruption, because so many schools follow the same books.
- **ICSE** is often described as giving more weight to English language and literature and to a wider spread of subjects in Classes 9 and 10. Some children enjoy that breadth; others find the load heavy.
- At Class 11 and 12, both boards offer science, commerce and humanities streams, and the differences narrow.

## Board examinations

- **CBSE** has moved to **two board examinations for Class 10**, from 2026: the first, in February, is compulsory, and a second, in May, is an optional chance to improve marks in some subjects. Check the CBSE website for the current rules.
- **CISCE** holds the ICSE and ISC examinations once a year. See cisce.org for the current scheme and dates.

> Board rules change. Before you decide, read the latest circulars on cbse.gov.in and cisce.org rather than relying on older articles.

## What matters more than the board

The National Education Policy 2020 asks all boards to move towards **understanding over memorisation**, so the gap between boards is narrowing. When you compare two schools, these questions usually decide your child's experience more than the board does:

- **Teachers and teaching:** how classes are taught, class size and how the school supports children who fall behind.
- **Distance:** a long daily journey takes time from rest, play and study.
- **Fees:** the full annual cost, not just tuition. Compare official fee schedules where schools publish them.
- **Facilities that matter to your child:** library, laboratories, sports, arts.
- **Continuity:** does the school go up to Class 12, and in the stream your child may want?
- **Your family's plans:** if you may move cities, a widely available board is easier.

## How to decide

1. List the schools you can realistically reach, then note each one's board.
2. Visit, or speak to parents, and ask the questions above.
3. If two good schools differ only by board, choose the one whose approach suits your child, and the one that is easier for your family to sustain.
